Build levels

Three levels. You choose how much you build.

Every level ends with a drone you assembled yourself. What changes is how much arrives ready-made and which tools you need on your bench.

Level 1 · Starter

Snap-fit and plug and play

Fromquad-copter

The quickest route from box to sky. Most of the chassis snaps together, a few screws hold the electronics, and every electrical connection is a plug.

What you do

  • Snap the chassis together.
  • Screw the flight controller to the ESC and to the drone.
  • Screw the rotors onto the drone.
  • Plug the battery into the ESC with its easy-connect plug.
  • Plug the rotors into the ESC.
  • Optionally plug in an FPV camera, a VTX and an ELRS receiver, which use the same plug-and-play connectors.

What you need

  • A precision screwdriver set.

Skill: none. No soldering.

Level 2 · Builder

Pre-made chassis and light soldering

Fromquad-copter

The chassis parts arrive ready-made. You take on your first soldering jobs: the plug-and-play joints that connect to the ESC, and the easy joints on the flight controller.

What you do

  • Fit the pre-made chassis parts together.
  • Solder the plug-and-play joints to the ESC.
  • Solder the FPV camera or VTX to the flight controller at the easy solder points.
  • Solder the ELRS receiver to the flight controller.
  • Finish the remaining mounting and connections.

What you need, on top of Level 1

  • A soldering iron.
  • Solder, flux and a helping-hands or board holder.
  • Wire strippers and cutters.
  • Heat shrink for insulating joints.
  • A multimeter is recommended.

Skill: a little soldering practice. A few practice joints on scrap wire is enough.

Level 3 · Pro

Components, wires and a CAD file

Fromquad-copter, CAD file included

You receive the components and wires with nothing soldered to any board, and no chassis. In its place you get a CAD file, plus extra parts you can add from the CAD hub to match your preferences.

What you do

  • Print the chassis from the CAD file, on your own printer or through a school, library or print service.
  • Choose extra parts from the CAD hub for your drone type.
  • Solder or crimp every connection yourself: battery lead, rotors to the ESC, FPV camera or VTX and ELRS receiver to the flight controller.
  • Test each connection, then configure and tune.

What you need, on top of Level 2

  • A temperature-controlled soldering station.
  • A crimping tool.
  • A 3D printer or access to one.
  • A multimeter, plus a hobby knife and small files for cleaning printed parts.

Skill: comfortable soldering, including small pads, and reading a wiring diagram.

Every levelA computer to configure the flight controller and radio, and a LiPo balance charger with a fire-safe bag for charging.

Pricing

Do more of the building, pay less.

Each level hands you more of the work, so it costs less. Fixed wing and VTOL aircraft cost more than quad-copters because they use more complex airframes and extra parts. All prices are in US dollars.

Schools & STEM organizations · DC, Maryland, Virginia

Bring hands-on drone engineering to your students.

We are looking to partner with schools and STEM enrichment organizations across the DMV. Students get to build the aircraft they fly, which makes engineering something they can see and test.

Every skill level, one program

The three build levels let a mixed group work side by side, from first-time builders to experienced students.

No lab required
